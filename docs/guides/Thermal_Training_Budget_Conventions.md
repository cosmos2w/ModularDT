# Thermal training budget conventions

Thermal Channel development uses the fixed input-selected TRAIN150 membership for affordable learning comparisons and all 600 native training cases for formal predictor/organizer evidence. Model evaluation still uses matched approximately 10% panels by default; training on all 600 cases does not authorize inference on all cases. An epoch visits the declared native membership once. Additional response supervision must be stated separately: several historical TRAIN150 recipes also fitted edited scenes outside that native membership. This guide governs planning; it does not authorize new training or override a user's narrower stop.

Policy revision, 14 November 2026: the user explicitly requested that evidence-based Thermal budget conventions become project-wide requirements. This revision supersedes the older rule that treated every subset comparison as qualification within one 500-epoch ceiling. The technical qualification ceiling remains; substantive learning is a separately declared allocation. Historical durations motivate these planning defaults and do not themselves grant execution or continuation authority.

## What the saved history supports

The 2026-11-14 audit read saved configurations, histories and contemporaneous reviews without training, model inference or physical solves. It resolved duplicate aliases and distinguished fresh global-model weights, inherited weights, technical checks, preliminary selection and substantive learning comparisons. The detailed local audit is named `Report_R17/R17-3/budget_history`; its numerical evidence lives separately in the round's `budget_audit` output directory. These local archives are not required to use this guide on another installation.

The explicitly enumerated TRAIN150 comparison cohort contains 29 fresh seed-zero lineages from nine detailed campaigns. It is a scoped historical sample, weighted by configurations rather than independent research questions; it is not an exhaustive or random survey. Eight of the 29 comparison ages were 500, one was 999 and twenty were 1,000; the median was **1,000 epochs**. Separate R13-10 and R15-2 comparisons made explicitly preliminary decisions at 100 and must not disappear from the history, but they do not establish adequate completed learning. R14-2 extended an inherited 1,000-epoch TRAIN150 trajectory to 2,000 with 1,000 new epochs; it was not a fifth fresh R14 model. Reusing a control or adding a warm child does not add an independent fresh training result.

A separate scoped cohort of 14 fresh-global-predictor TRAIN600 lineages from the selected primary R14, R15-2, R16-1, R16-2, R16-10 and R17-2 comparisons had actual stops distributed as follows: five at 100, one at 503, four at 1,000, one at 2,500, two at 3,000 and one at 5,000. Its median actual stop was **1,000**, and eight reached at least 1,000. The 503 stop followed a scientific review at 500. These are authorization and disposition outcomes, not measured times to convergence. Four additional warm children added 2,500 new epochs and are excluded from that fresh-model distribution; an inherited age of 5,100 is not a fresh 100-epoch result.

Three measured changes explain why staging matters:

- In R17-2 TRAIN150, the native-observation response prescription with its response-gradient norm matched at the epoch-10 TRAIN-only calibration and the functional-contrast prescription had mean nine-field error ratios of 0.7683 and 0.9516 to their dense physical-field response control at 500. At 1,000 the ratios were 0.8497 and 0.8401: their order reversed. The calibration matched one weighted gradient norm, not its direction or sustained influence. The ratios describe physical RMSE on the same small exposed validation panel, not the sampled training loss.
- In R17-2 TRAIN600, the functional-contrast recipe's mean nine-field ratio to its control changed from 1.133 at 500 to 0.8768 at 1,000. An early field deficit did not predict its later native-prediction role. Its response results remained mixed, so this did not establish a general response or design replacement.
- In R16-10 TRAIN600, continuing from 2,000 to 3,000 worsened the response recipe's mean native error ratio to its same-age native control from 1.216 to 1.317. More epochs did not resolve that tradeoff. In R14-2, the extra 1,000 TRAIN150 epochs improved nine mean field errors by 4.4–27.4% while still failing to reproduce the mature full-data organization behavior.

## Default stages and decisions

| Purpose | Planned horizon and reviews | What the stage can decide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Execution/gradient/recovery qualification | Only the epochs necessary; at most 500 aggregate technical epochs per round unless changed explicitly | Whether the implemented path and recovery work; no quality ranking |
| Substantive TRAIN150 learning comparison | Normally target 1,000 per configuration; review by 100, at 500 and at 1,000 | At 100, learning health/cost; at 500, real tradeoffs and bounded allocation; at 1,000, subset selection or closure |
| Specific TRAIN150 follow-up | An explicitly budgeted same-lineage extension toward 2,000, with an intermediate review where useful | One unresolved learning/schedule/mechanism question; no automatic tie-breaking campaign |
| Initial TRAIN600 segment | Normally 100 per selected fresh model | Preliminary full-data evidence and operational handoff; user continuation decision when required |
| Authorized substantial TRAIN600 comparison | Review at 500 and 1,000 | Intermediate and substantial full-data predictor/organizer/response decisions against matched controls |
| Declared longer TRAIN600 question | Review at 2,000 when justified by the schedule or trajectory | Whether the specific remaining learning question changes the choice; no default 3,000–5,000 extension |

The 100, 500 and 1,000 reviews are cumulative ages within one learning lineage: its first 100 and next 400 are already included in its 1,000-epoch total. Count a separate qualification or failed/retried fit once in actual work and its declared reserve; do not charge the embedded health review a second time or erase interrupted fitting. New work on a warm child is reported alongside its inherited age.

At 500, normally continue a healthy intended TRAIN150 comparison to its declared 1,000. A code error, divergence, unusable resource cost or a persistent measured tradeoff that answers the question can justify an earlier stop; record that reason and save the state. One small-panel loss or a missed heuristic band is not a new blocking gate. A tied result at the planned endpoint is a result to disposition, not automatic permission to train longer. A subset winner is a nomination for fresh full-data work, not a reliable full-data ranking: the R17-2 native-observation response prescription's ratio to its own control changed from 0.768 at TRAIN150-500 to 1.347 at TRAIN600-500. The two stages also fitted their own scales and exposures; this is evidence that the ranking did not transfer, not proof of a population-size effect alone.

## Operational release and scientific adoption are different

A 100-epoch TRAIN600 review can release an already authorized continuation without continued conversational supervision when the intended native and auxiliary objectives have actually run, losses and gradients are finite, checkpoint restoration preserves model/optimizer/RNG/sampler state, curves are present, and the measured memory/cost supports the remaining work. Use existing records and focused verification; do not add a new monitoring service or admission framework. If the intended objective has not run by 100, say so and diagnose it rather than calling the path qualified.

This operational decision does not say that field errors, physical changes or inverse designs are stable. State quality needs its matched physical errors; response quality needs differences for the declared changed inputs at corresponding physical positions; design quality needs its declared physical acceptance checks. Report them separately after adequate training on all TRAIN600 members, using the declared matched approximately 10% evaluation panels by default. The historical 1,000-epoch median is a useful planning anchor, not a convergence guarantee.

The project normally grants only the first 100 epochs initially. Its existing exception permits one explicitly proposed final formal candidate to continue to 500 after a recorded review, within the round budget; it does not authorize all controls or any later extension. The user's latest narrower restriction always wins. **For R17-3, each selected TRAIN600 run stops at exactly 100 and only the user can approve continuation.** No monitoring agent, completion hook or launcher can extend it.

## Fair stages, costs and explicit bounds

Declare the small set of changed operations, fresh/warm initialization, native membership, auxiliary exposure, per-configuration target, review points, aggregate new epochs, repair reserve and stopping/adoption question before launch. Preserve the same lineage and its optimizer/RNG/sampling state between reviews. Keep the learning-rate schedule separate from each executable segment stop; shortening or restarting its schedule at 100 or 500 changes the experiment. Any intentional schedule change must be stated as such.

Use matched approximately 10% model-evaluation panels by default and preserve their IDs across compared models; keep detailed fields/organization to the small declared representative panel. A staged review can read all saved scalar history without running new full-population inference. Save curves when history first exists, every 10 epochs, at validation, and at each pause/final stop; keep the ordinary validation cadence and useful numerical history. Reuse unchanged saved evaluations.

Forecast cost from comparable saved process occupancy, then reforecast once from actual startup timing. Include preparation, calibration, validation, recording and finalization; record training-only timers separately. Sum device occupancy across concurrent jobs: two devices can reduce elapsed time without reducing aggregate device-hours. Hardware, objective and sampling differences must be disclosed; do not claim acceleration from these historical cross-run costs.

For orientation, R17-1's two 500-epoch TRAIN150 fitting processes occupied about 5.55 aggregate GPU-hours, or about 20 seconds per epoch. R17-2's screen charged about 19.71 process GPU-hours for 3,500 aggregate TRAIN150 epochs plus its recorded interruption/review costs, again about 20 seconds per fitted epoch at that accounting scope. R17-2's two formal first-100 segments occupied about 4.20 process GPU-hours combined; its two 1,000-epoch formal processes occupied about 37.18 combined. These are local recipe/hardware observations, not portable wall-time promises. A new native-only recipe may cost less, and a different auxiliary prescription may cost more.

For a three-configuration 1,000-epoch TRAIN150 study plus two TRAIN600 first-100 segments, those historical rates imply about 21 aggregate process GPU-hours before a small repair/evaluation allowance. Do not replace the user's requested substantive comparison with 150 epochs merely to satisfy an invented 10-hour ceiling, and do not respond by proposing an open-ended 100-hour campaign. Bound the actual configurations and epochs, estimate their cost honestly, and surface a material forecast change at a planned review. GPU selection belongs in runtime configuration; a permitted device change does not require a new scientific freeze.
