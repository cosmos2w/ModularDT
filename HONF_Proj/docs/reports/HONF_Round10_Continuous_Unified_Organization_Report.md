**Gains.** On the 22-case exposed Thermal DEV panel at e5000, C10-S's mean fluid/surface/material temperature RMSE is 0.617683/0.700755/0.738676 in dataset temperature units, respectively 10.26%/6.71%/7.10% below same-age dense M+E. These are equal-case physical errors, without training-objective weights.

**Misses.** Thermal C10-H's mean surface-temperature RMSE is 0.882010, 17.43% above dense M+E, and module-peak RMSE is 1.011507, 13.04% above that control. Both miss the prospective +10% mean band. Its surface-temperature M3 and M10 strata, and material-temperature/module-peak M10 strata, exceed the +25% investigation band against dense M+E. The corresponding surface-temperature M3 stratum also exceeds that band against dense M. Overall means therefore do not establish preservation in every populated category. Mature Thermal S9-ME's module-peak mean is 13.01% above dense M+E, and mature Wind H9-ME's background mean is 10.71% above that control. Against same-age dense M, none of these DEV means misses the +10% band. No priority DEV p90 comparison in this final six-arm inventory misses the +15% band against either dense baseline. Ordinary misses remain measured outcomes; no trajectory was erased or extended to avoid them.

**Gains.** The trained continuous readers execute selected original M/E value rows with exact-positive access, and the saved native counters measure those reads. At e5000 Thermal DEV, C10-H retains 85.809% of valid-present M pairs and 32.059% of valid-present E pairs; C10-S retains 83.308% and 60.976%. Wind C10-H retains 100.000% M and 59.525% E; C10-S retains 84.017% M and 73.863% E. Wind C10-H therefore achieves no M-row sparsity at this endpoint. Small positive coefficients still count as complete selected value rows.

**Misses and attribution limits.** Neither trained H/S pair overlaps within five percentage points in both typed retention rates on either TRAIN or DEV. For continuous Thermal DEV the M difference is only 2.501 percentage points, but E differs by 28.917 points; on Wind the differences are 15.983 M and 14.337 E points. The final H-versus-S accuracy comparison therefore cannot isolate a benefit from hypergraph grouping at matched work. Exact-count interventions, applied coefficient traces, and rewiring tests must be interpreted with their own measured final M/E work. Applied learned paths can establish executed information flow; they cannot establish physical causal edges.

## Computation efficiency and correctness

Measured value-row reduction, saved-tensor memory, complete native-cycle elapsed time, and inclusive engineering-operation time are separate quantities. All-open M+E is a different mathematical control from the selected trained function. A same-mask dense-value companion instead tests executor agreement while retaining the trained selected coefficients. Neither comparison may substitute for the other.

All eight full four-epoch engineering cycles have completed: selected and all-open controls for Thermal H/S and Wind H/S. Each pair restores the same literal endpoint model, Adam, provider, RNG, and ordered input cases; each Thermal operation discards 16 updates and each Wind operation discards 20. The complete native selected cycles take 148.061/110.490/84.422/79.943 seconds for Thermal H/S and Wind H/S, respectively, versus 67.672/55.542/36.259/36.613 seconds for their all-open controls. Selected execution is 98.93–132.83% slower in these measured replays. No runtime saving is established.

## Deferred U: evidence and next-stage decision

**Current decision.** U training is deferred, with zero U scientific training epochs and no trained-performance claim. Preserve the implementation, local-record interfaces, two contextual MM→EM→EE→ME cycles, and six previously completed one-epoch engineering qualifications. Those qualifications establish bounded startup/cost feasibility on their bound source and device, with discarded updates; they do not establish convergence, reconstruction quality, organizer quality, or useful inverse designs. Further U-specific GPU qualification and automatic successors remain disabled.

**Scientific questions and controls.** A separately approved stage should test whether applied typed local-record preparation preserves field reconstruction after removing the old aggregate/global broadcast paths; whether contextual organization helps beyond trained U-S; and whether exact-positive retrieval provides a useful accuracy/work/cost tradeoff. Use complete matched U-D/U-H/U-S triplets with identical fixed input-only TRAIN/DEV memberships, normalization, parents, seeds, losses, query schedules, and horizons. Compare U-D with the new all-open U equations rather than requiring equality to an intentionally different old dense predictor.

**Correctness and go/no-go.** Before launch, verify source/checkpoint/data lineage, target/reference poisoning, both contextual stage orders and applied incidences, disconnected-component value dependence with frozen coefficients, E-conditioned M responses, exact-zero and empty-support boundaries, and packed/dense forward-and-gradient agreement. Qualify legal geometry derivatives with rebuilt scenes and physical-grid limits. Go requires separate user authorization, complete triplet plus evaluation/storage allocation, and all identity/correctness checks at fixed tolerances; no-go includes missing lineage, hidden value paths, numerical failure, incomplete controls, or insufficient complete allocation. Ordinary accuracy or speed misses are reported outcomes, not a reason to silently change the model or extend a horizon.

**Estimated cost.** The saved one-epoch qualifications imply about 78.71 GPU-associated fitting hours and 54,000 main updates for both complete 2,000-epoch triplets using the existing forecast formula. A preliminary separate 2-hour correctness and 8-hour evaluation allowance brings the scenario to approximately 88.71 associated hours. The storage proxy is 16 GiB retained and 4 GiB temporary, with a 100 GiB free-space floor and no dataset/cache copies. These are planning estimates, not authorization or measured full-horizon costs.
