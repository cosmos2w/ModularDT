## Current hard limits

- The physical reference is a local analytic-wake/shared-grid generator; its pressure and flux conventions are documented, but it does not supply Navier–Stokes CFD credibility or a deployment pressure allowance. The `1.05 × baseline` pressure boundary is a frozen experimental benchmark.
- The two-layout Re90 crossed-context panel reveals essentially invariant finite responses in this generator. Its changed pressure feasibility comes from the context-dependent baseline; it does not establish general Reynolds transfer of physical interactions.
- The nominal layout trial is a single train-exposed family. A predeclared group and a physically useful joint move do not establish a learned adaptive graph's design utility.
- The local move changes sharply across the two grids. Neither the nominal comparison nor a two-grid pass licenses continuous shape-gradient optimization at this move scale.
- Exact native adapter parity and broad untrained replay are established. The two completed matched u100 response reviews did not deliver decision-useful finite-response recovery; they do not prove a frozen local-model capacity floor.
- The first raw-index and the bounded two-layout role-balanced WindFarm oracles both retained full access as the measured execution choice. Teacher-adequate partial root prunes were slower than policy-free Dense, no adequate split was found, and the input-only organizer fit target remained unavailable after the permitted index/cache remedies. This panel cannot establish case-dependent K or native design utility. Dense coarse/local paths still carry layout information outside the fine cover.

## Decision and evidence that would change it

Retain the intact Run1804 absolute predictor and **per-module, pressure baseline correction** for bounded, independently checked Thermal proposals; retain Run1502 as the separate fixed-heat and finite-response control. The tested native output-head response updates are not a decision-useful replacement. The unresolved M10 gradient conflict and P0/P2 intervention identify where to inspect the local path; neither establishes a capacity theorem.

Retain policy-free Dense execution for native WindFarm inference. The adaptive tree, real row masks and input-only organizer code remain a research instrument, but the bounded oracle found no cheaper coherent partial label to fit and the consequential coarse/local routes still bypass fine-cover decisions. Revisit learned covers only after directly measured native partial execution beats Dense on independent layouts while retaining disjoint teacher fidelity; then test any claimed physical graph against independent reference quantities and the coarse/local ablations. Larger inference tiles or batched source blocks are possible measured-work remedies, not results of this round.

Retain the matched reference-corrected inverse protocol as a **train-exposed negative control**. Its nominated joint move was feasible and improved the peak on both nominal and doubled reference grids, but the local comparator changed sharply with grid resolution and the group was not learned from a native adaptive graph. A design-utility claim needs a held-out objective-relevant panel with a reliable pressure-boundary contrast and numerically resolved selected moves, under the same candidate and reference-call budgets. ## External-reference information needed for a stronger WindFarm design claim

The stored native velocity fields support finite-library and teacher-preservation studies. A new-layout physical-response claim needs the external solver or generator and its runnable input/output contract: the coordinate frame and wind-direction transform; rotor or actuator geometry and force convention; controllable turbine variables; inflow, outlet, terrain and turbulence boundary conditions; and the definition of the scalar design objective, including any power/AEP calculation. Until those are available, a turbine-neighborhood velocity statistic remains a velocity statistic, and `wake_loss_pct` remains a stored empirical label rather than a derived power law.
