# Shared interaction core, environmental flow context, and receiver packets

**Bounded development study, 7 October 2026 — final report.** This round extracts the actual source/environment context computation and geometric receiver reads into shared code. Thermal uses the retained affine heating reader; a fresh Wind model uses source-wise nonlinear messages followed by a nonlinear velocity head. The datasets execute the same implementation with different weights, input transforms and output laws. Native Thermal extraction, zero benchmark temperature offset, heating units and the triangular flow-to-temperature dependency remain Thermal-owned.

The matched Thermal experiment continues the development R-direct 2500 parent on the unchanged fixed25_v1 subset. R-geom keeps geometry-only preparation; R-flowctx adds predicted physical u/v from the same frozen development D-sep 2500, through a zero-initialized projection. It never reads target flow as inference context. The experiment tests whether this additional information improves temperature, rather than treating the new dependency edge itself as evidence of improvement.

The receiver interface exports physical donor sets for receiver regions, keeps global source/environment ancestry explicit, and preserves the exact baseline while approximating only declared fixed-geometry increments. One small TRAIN-only scorer amortizes the frozen reconstruction model's raw source sensitivities. Exact covers and equal-K geometry controls test its usefulness separately from physical response error; experimental subset execution is explicitly priced and full access remains the default. No latent bank, forced donor count, physical-causality claim or inverse design campaign is introduced.

The real Wind pilot completed 2000 optimizer updates on 24 original TRAIN layouts and eight original validation layouts. It improves the five-role validation objective from 183.571 at update 1000 to 141.898 at update 2000 and beats the TRAIN-fitted height-only profile control (417.464). The retained Wind 2103 e2475 reference is much stronger (1.23874) and has unequal training history. The two native velocity planes visibly show the pilot's broad-flow learning and missed turbine wakes. This supports implementation portability and bounded learning feasibility, not mature Wind accuracy or shared-weight transfer.

The result to retain is the compatible shared implementation and the qualified prepared-response API. Keep the mature/formal predictors as scientific references and R-geom 1200 as this study's development teacher. Flow context does not earn promotion. The learned packet matches exact selection at equal donor counts and improves teacher fidelity over geometry heuristics, but retains all donors in most patches and misses the partial-use target. Its fitted margins remain empirical. The Wind result earns a shared-code learning proof, with substantial remaining wake error. No additional training or physical-reference campaign follows from this result.

## Shared implementation and numerical contract

`InteractionContextCore` owns the original two source/source and source/environment rounds, normalized physical-measure reductions, Fourier geometry and receiver/source feature construction. `SourceResponseOperator` inherits these functions without nesting or renaming checkpoint tensors. `NonlinearFieldReadout` executes an individual source-message MLP before reducing across physical sources, then a nonlinear field head; environment enters through prepared context/global state, with no dense receiver/environment fine stack. The measured runtime trace and source hashes establish shared execution, beyond a common facade. The [maintained guide](../../guides/Shared_Interaction_Core_and_Receiver_Packets.md) documents the dataset-owned contracts and public entry points.

At unchanged development-parent weights, the extracted Thermal path preserves every state key and produces bitwise-identical outputs and heat/geometry VJPs against the original implementation on two input-selected TRAIN layouts and the four fixed DEV representatives, at Q1024/Q8192. Physical-source permutations, environment-measure splitting, precise increments and cold/prepared agreement pass. A separate CUDA-hidden CPU check strictly loads the retained development R-group e2400 and compares the original/group and shared/group implementations on the same six native layouts at Q1024/Q8192, including all-role heat/geometry VJPs; state keys and outputs agree at unchanged tolerances. The separately timed R-direct e2500 CUDA 0 comparison has median complete-inference ratio 1.009876 and incremental-peak-allocation ratio 1.0, inside the declared 10% engineering ceiling; the CUDA-hidden R-group receipt establishes parity only. These measurements are in [direct compatibility](../../../diagnostics/generated/shared_interaction_20261007/compatibility.json) and [group parity](../../../diagnostics/generated/shared_interaction_20261007/compatibility_group_cpu.json). Complete timing includes input staging, composed preparation, all native output roles and transfer; it excludes checkpoint and HDF5 loading. Both comparison sides use the common updated native adapter and validation, so this ratio isolates core-extraction overhead; the full current-adapter costs are separately measured below. Existing FP32 query-chunk flux/proxy failures remain recorded at their original tolerance; extraction does not silently turn them into passes. FP64 accumulation improves increment arithmetic, not learned physical fidelity.

`DependencySpec` rejects cyclic dependencies and an applicable affine control that reaches prepared context. Thermal heating acts after coefficient preparation; changing geometry or prescribed context rebuilds it. Wind configuration enters its nonlinear preparation and exact affine finite-increment calls are rejected; local AD JVPs carry their operating point and physical velocity transform. Prepared requests bind tensor versions, parameter identities/versions and operator ownership. Packets additionally bind physical source IDs, ordered receiver IDs and coordinates, scene/action fingerprints, units and calibrated scorer weights. Geometry changes require a fresh exact baseline; stale proposals can then fall back to full access. Optimizer updates, weight reloads, changed TRAIN normalization or moved receivers invalidate the old state. Final review repaired a composed record-increment path that bypassed frozen-flow freshness and added normalization bindings for both endpoint and increment reads. Successful reads followed by flow/statistic mutation, rejection and fresh rebuild are tested; numerical kernels and trained checkpoints are unchanged.

## Receiver packets and one learned proposer

For each fixed4×4 fluid patch, raw sensitivity is the normalized-quadrature RMS of the frozen model's individual K column. Admissible-action importance multiplies that sensitivity by the corresponding heating-change radius. Their meanings remain separate: a nearly saturated actuator can have a small omission score despite a strong raw response. The exact triangle omission bound controls the learned affine model within the declared action box; it is not a physical-reference bound. The canonical patch catalogue is fixed input quadrature, not a learned spatial partition.

The single scorer is H32 with 6689 trainable parameters, seed 0 and 500 CPU epochs (3000 optimizer updates). Exact frozen-teacher tables cover all 150 primary TRAIN layouts; 128 fit the log-sensitivity model and 22 original-TRAIN layouts form the whole-layout calibration holdout. The floor and residual margin are TRAIN-derived. Log loss falls 0.08022→0.03629. Training itself takes 3.397 s, the fitted/calibrated function 3.762 s, and table reads/exports plus fit 7.073 s; wrapper/import time is reported in the final ledger. Its input-only features append permitted radius divided by mean prescribed active heating to the shared source/global/environment and geometric features. Thus its supervised raw s_i label is K-only, but the learned estimate can depend on action-radius metadata; only deterministic cover selection forms r_i times predicted sensitivity. No target flow enters the features.
